# From Three Staff to Ten: Where AI in Construction Fits as a Trade Business Grows

Growth in a trade or building business rarely breaks the work itself. It breaks the admin around the work. The owner who could juggle everything with three people suddenly finds that with eight, things fall through gaps nobody can see. That's often the point where AI in construction moves from curiosity to something worth looking at seriously. But where it fits changes at each stage of growth.

## Stage 1: Owner-operator plus one or two

**What it looks like:** the owner quotes, works on the tools, answers the phone and does the paperwork at night. A partner or family member might help with the books.

**What breaks first:** availability. The owner can't answer calls and work at the same time, and quotes go out late at night and are rarely followed up.

**Where agents fit:** answering calls during the working day and capturing enquiries properly. Follow-up on quotes that the owner would never get around to chasing.

**What to avoid:** over-engineering. At this stage you need one agent doing one job well.

## Stage 2: First office hire

**What it looks like:** four to six people. Someone now runs the office, at least part-time. The owner is starting to step off the tools.

**What breaks next:** the office person becomes the bottleneck. Every call, every follow-up, every document goes through them, and they're constantly interrupted.

**Where agents fit:** taking the repetitive load off the office person, particularly paperwork chasing and overflow calls, so they can focus on clients, scheduling and invoicing.

**What to avoid:** introducing agents without the office person involved. They hold the knowledge that makes the setup work.

## Stage 3: First project manager or estimator

**What it looks like:** six to ten people. Multiple crews. Someone other than the owner is now quoting or running jobs.

**What breaks next:** consistency. Different people follow up quotes differently, or not at all. Paperwork requirements differ by client and by job, and nobody has the full picture.

**Where agents fit:** standardising follow-up so every quote gets the same treatment, regardless of who sent it. Chasing compliance documents across multiple jobs and subcontractors. For businesses moving into commercial work, first drafts of SWMS, JSAs and RAMS, reviewed by a competent person.

**What to avoid:** letting each person set up their own tools. Agents should work from the shared job software, not from individual inboxes.

## Stage 4: Multiple crews and bigger projects

**What it looks like:** ten people and beyond. Larger contracts, head contractors, and perhaps a move from simpler job software to something like simPRO or Procore.

**What breaks next:** contract risk and coordination. More is at stake in each agreement, and more documents move between more parties.

**Where agents fit:** a first read of contracts to flag clauses for closer human review, and coordinated paperwork chasing across subcontractors and suppliers.

**What to avoid:** assuming agents replace a contracts or safety person on larger jobs. They support them.

## A pattern across the stages

At every stage, the same three jobs come up: answering calls, chasing paperwork and following up quotes. What changes is who they're taking load off: first the owner, then the office person, then the project team. The smart move is to add agents just before a stage breaks, not after the mess has built up.
