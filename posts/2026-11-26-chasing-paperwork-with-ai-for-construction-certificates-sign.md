# Chasing Paperwork With AI for Construction: Certificates, Sign-Offs and Supplier Documents

Ask builders where admin time disappears and 'chasing' comes up again and again, which makes paperwork one of the most practical targets for AI for construction. Not writing documents, but getting them from other people: the compliance certificate from the sparky, the signed variation from the client, the insurance certificate from a new subbie, the delivery docket the supplier swears they sent.

## Why chasing is so draining

Chasing has three properties that make it hard for busy people:

- **It depends on someone else.** You can't finish it by working harder.
- **It needs repeating.** One request rarely does it; you need a second and a third.
- **It's easy to forget.** It stays out of sight until it suddenly blocks a payment or a handover.

That combination is why documents go missing for weeks. Nobody decided to let it slide. It just never became the most urgent thing on any particular day.

## The usual suspects

Every business has its own list, but these show up often:

**From clients:** signed acceptances, signed variations, access details, selections and finishes.

**From subcontractors:** insurance certificates, licences, signed safety documents, completion photos, compliance certificates.

**From suppliers:** delivery confirmations, warranties, product data, corrected invoices.

**From certifiers and authorities:** inspection bookings and outcomes, approvals.

It's worth writing your own version. Most owners are surprised how long it is.

## What makes a good chaser

Whether a person or an agent sends it, a good chaser is:

- **Specific.** 'The electrical compliance certificate for the Smith St job' beats 'the paperwork'.
- **Polite but firm.** Explain why you need it and by when.
- **Easy to act on.** Tell them exactly how to send it back.
- **Scheduled.** A set rhythm of reminders rather than whenever you remember.
- **Escalating.** After a set number of tries, a person takes over.

## Where an agent helps

This is a task an agent handles well because the logic is clear: a document is due, it hasn't arrived, send a reminder, note it, try again later, and flag it to a person if it still hasn't come. It doesn't get embarrassed about asking a third time, and it doesn't forget on a busy Thursday.

What it shouldn't do is accept a document as correct without someone checking it, or make threats about payment or contract terms on your behalf. Keep those calls human.
