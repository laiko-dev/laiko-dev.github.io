# Seven Days to Live: Your Side of an AI for Trades Rollout

Some providers of ai for trades can get agents running within a week. laikodev, for example, goes live in 7 days after a free intake call. That's quick, which is good, but it also means the week goes fast. The businesses that get the most out of a rollout are the ones that use those seven days well on their own side.

This is a day-by-day plan for the owner or office manager. It doesn't describe any provider's internal process. It's about what you can do in parallel so that day seven is a clean start rather than a scramble.

## Day 1: Tell the team

Before anything goes live, tell everyone who touches the phone, quotes or paperwork what's changing and why. Field staff especially should know that a customer might mention 'the person who answered' and that it was the agent.

Keep it simple: calls you miss will be answered, quotes will get followed up, paperwork will get chased, and anything tricky comes back to a person.

## Day 2: Write down your urgent list

Decide what counts as an emergency for your business and who gets contacted for it. Write it as a short, strict list. A gas smell, an uncontrollable leak, a danger to someone in the home. If you're a builder, maybe a site safety issue or a client who's very upset.

Also write down who is on call, and their backup.

## Day 3: Tidy the job software

Agents built around your job software, whether that's ServiceM8, Tradify, simPRO or Procore, work best when what's in there is current. Spend an hour on:

- Closing old quotes that are dead
- Marking won and lost jobs correctly
- Fixing obviously wrong customer contact details

No need for perfection. Just make sure the agent won't chase someone you lost six months ago.

## Day 4: Decide your follow-up preferences

For quotes and paperwork, note how persistent you want to be. How long after sending a quote should the first check-in happen? How many follow-ups before it's closed off? Which customers, such as regular builders you work with, should be handled differently?

Also note the hard lines: no discounting, no promising dates, no discussing disputes.

## Day 5: Prepare your phone setup

Work out the practical bit: which calls go to the agent and when. All calls? Only ones you don't answer within a few rings? Only after hours? Check how call forwarding works on your phone or business line, so you're not working it out on the day.

## Day 6: Write three test calls

Think of three realistic calls and have someone you trust make them once the agent is ready:

- A routine new job enquiry
- A genuine emergency from your urgent list
- A slightly awkward one, like an existing customer unhappy about a delay

What you're checking is whether the details captured are the ones you'd want, and whether the urgent call reaches the right person.

## Day 7: Go live, then watch

On the first day, read every captured call and every follow-up sent. It takes a few minutes. Note anything that should be phrased differently or handled another way, and feed it back.

For the first couple of weeks, keep checking daily. After that, weekly is usually enough.

## What makes a week enough

A short rollout works because the agents fit around what you already have. You're not migrating to new job software or retraining staff on a new system. The work on your side is mostly writing down decisions you already make in your head.
